M extension: multiply and divide¶

The M extension adds eight integer instructions to RV32I: MUL, MULH, MULHSU, MULHU, DIV, DIVU, REM and REMU. They share the OP opcode with the base arithmetic instructions and are marked by funct7 = 0000001. ORCA executes them in one unit, the MDU (rtl/cpu/6-execute/mdu.sv), which has a pipelined multiplier and an iterative divider. This note follows an M instruction from decode to the result bus.

Path through the pipeline¶

Decode accepts funct7 = MULDIV as a legal R-type form and keeps funct7 in the instruction packet, so the MDU can tell the eight operations apart. Rename gives the instruction a physical destination and a ROB entry like any register-writing instruction. Dispatch writes it into the scheduler with the multiply/divide class.

The scheduler issues M instructions on port 2, which also serves memory address generation and CSR work. When several entries are ready on that port, the oldest ready memory entry goes first; otherwise the oldest ready multiply/divide or CSR entry goes. An M instruction reads both source registers in the auxiliary register-read stage, which adds one issue-to-execute cycle compared with an ALU instruction. The unit accepts a new operation only when it has room, and the scheduler sees that through the port 2 ready signal.

The MDU is execute slot 4. Its result reaches the common data bus through the writeback arbiter (Stage 7) as an ordinary register write: it wakes dependent instructions, writes the physical register file, and marks the ROB entry complete. M instructions raise no exceptions. Division by zero and signed overflow return defined values.

Multiply¶

A multiply has four cycles from issue to visible result and the unit accepts one new multiply per cycle. The registered path is the input packet, the partial products, the recombination, and the held result.

The multiplier works on 33-bit signed operands. A leading zero makes an operand unsigned and a repeated sign bit makes it signed, so a single product covers MUL, MULH, MULHSU and MULHU with no absolute-value adders in front of the multiplier and no 64-bit negate behind it. One operand is split at bit 17:

b = b_lo + (b_hi << 17)

Two narrower products are registered, then sign-extended and recombined into the exact 66-bit two’s-complement result. The split keeps a full 33 by 33 multiplier out of a single cycle. The low or high 32 bits of the result are selected by the operation.

Divide and remainder¶

The divider is an iterative restoring divider with signed fixup: it takes absolute values, runs a shift-subtract loop that produces one quotient bit per cycle for 32 iterations, and corrects the signs on the way out. It records its busy state, a 6-bit iteration count, the quotient, the remainder, the divisor, the sign-fixup flags, the result selection, and the destination tag. Only the busy flag and the count are reset; the data registers are loaded when a divide starts. One negator serves the result: the quotient or remainder is selected first and then negated if its sign flag is set.

The two special cases need no path of their own, because the loop already produces the architectural results. With a zero divisor every step subtracts nothing, so every quotient bit is one and the remainder ends as the dividend; the quotient’s sign flip is turned off for a zero divisor, so a signed divide by zero also returns all ones. The most negative number divided by minus one becomes 0x8000_0000 divided by one with matching signs, which gives the most negative number and a zero remainder. Both cases take the full 32 iterations.

Only one divide is in flight at a time, and it starts after older multiply state has drained. Younger MDU operations wait at issue until the divide completes or is killed.

Flush and kill¶

A mispredict kills younger work selectively. In the MDU, kill clears the validity of the in-flight operation and leaves the data path untouched; gating the multiplier payload on a ROB-age compare would put a wide compare in front of every product bit. A killed multiply or divide never writes a register or completes a ROB entry.

Limits¶

  • The divider is not pipelined, so a second divide waits for the first.

Tests¶

mdu_tb and execute_tb in sim/cpu/tb/6-execute/ check the multiply variants, the divide special cases, back-to-back issue, and kill. The RISC-V architecture tests for M (mul, mulh, mulhsu, mulhu, div, divu, rem, remu) pass through cpu_tb.
