Results¶

  • CoreMark: 1,032 per second at 200 MHz, 5.16 per MHz, IPC 1.51. The board log prints 1,090 because CoreMark rounds its run time down to whole seconds; the cycle count gives 1,032.

  • Embench-IoT: 2.31 per MHz over all 19 programs, 461 per second at 200 MHz.

  • Branch prediction: 98.9% of conditional branches right on CoreMark, 2.1 mispredictions per thousand instructions.

  • Architecture tests: all 67 pass, and each retires exactly the instructions the simulation retires.

  • Linux: no-MMU kernel 6.18.7 reaches a shell with 256 MiB of RAM. 40 of 40 boots finished the scripted session.

All the board numbers come from the same prebuilt bitstream and images that ship in the kit, run over JTAG with nothing running on the Arm side. Benchmarks are GCC 15.2 at -O3.

Linux¶

No-MMU Linux 6.18.7 boots to a BusyBox shell with 256 MiB of RAM. The kernel reaches /init after 1.53 s of kernel time at 200 MHz. How the image is built and loaded is on the Linux bring-up page.

Timing and size¶

The full SoC closes at 5.0 ns with no failing endpoints. Placement settings move this netlist anywhere from -0.56 to 0.000 ns, so I try several and keep the one that closes. The only on-chip RAM is the L1 caches while programs and data live in the board’s DDR.

Core Comparison¶

To get a better idea on how the core performs, I compared ORCA with three of the best/common open-source out-of-order RISC-V cores that I could build and run under similar conditions: NaxRiscv (SpinalHDL), RSD (SystemVerilog) and BOOM (Chisel). I also threw in Arm’s published Cortex-A9 figure as an additional datapoint. I’m sure there are other open cores available but these are the one that I found that are commonly known and can be built as a 2-wide OoO core. Every open core runs the same CoreMark and Embench sources with GCC 15.2 at -O3 and build the same out-of-context Vivado flow on the KV260 part. The scope is the core plus its L1 caches.

The results show that our core arguably performs the best in its 2-wide superscalar class, with Nax being the most performant and best 1-1 comparison. ORCA is 10.2% faster in Coremark/s and 4.0% faster on Embench/Mhz then Nax but unfortunately 2.38x larger. This is likely due to our routing logic and larger TAGE based predictor.

ASIC estimate¶

While the primary implementation target is FPGA, The core and L1 caches was also synthesized using Yosys to meet 1 GHz on ASAP7 standard cells before layout in about 0.081 mm². It’s only an estimate without placement or routing, and fake RAM timing. The timing, area and sizing reports are in results/asap7/, and the flow is on the bring-up page.

Performance, Area, & Timing Tuning¶

In order to stay competitive, we targeted frequency by reducing structure size tuned by the benchmarks and frequency gains and increased IPC on non critical paths. Utilization managment came well over our competition and so seeking a reduction of half was simply not plausable without major redesign. RTL alone got us to 175 MHz.

At 200MHz we were pushing the limits and trade-offs that set the core’s size and clock. Past 190 MHz every failing path was a complete loop that couldnt be pipelined in half or significanty reduced in structure size to offset routing delay.

RTL timing changes got us from 175 to about 190 MHz, but reaching 200MHz from there simply was EDA tool luck rather then an RTL issue. Remap optimization with net-delay placement and 0.2 ns of extra margin as well as letting LLMs perform a greedy search on all build directive combinations let us close at 200MHz after a weeks long search with each build lasting approximately an hour on a Ryzen 9800x3d. This is a significant milestone as we reach similar frequencies to Nax at even double the size. Most builds closed around 190MHz with good settings.

PULP IP vs Xilinx IP¶

For the SoC components I chose PULP IP for the AXI crossbar and UART 16550. I also attempted to use Xilinx’s IP which saved about 3,000 LUTs with no performance variance, however I was unable to close 200MHz out of 20 builds due to seed/placement variance. I did NOT want to do another week’s worth of searching. PULP IP was kept. It might also be useful if I ever plan on doing the full ASIC flow.
